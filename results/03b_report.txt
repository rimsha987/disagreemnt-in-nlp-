(1) IS THE DIRECTION OF THE SHIFT SYSTEMATIC?
================================================================================================
   The filter's MAGNITUDE was null (mean JSD p_perm=0.45). JSD is unsigned, so it
   cannot see a small consistent push. Testing the signed shift against the same
   null: remove 19 raters at random, 10,000 times.

   observed mean per-item change in P(Yes): +0.004519
   observed mean ABSOLUTE change:           0.011873
   observed majority-label flips:           9

   -- mean d_P(Yes) (signed) --
      null mean +0.000003   sd 0.007660   95% range [-0.015591, +0.014355]
      observed  +0.004519   z = +0.59   two-sided p_perm = 0.5635

   -- mean |d_P(Yes)| --
      null mean +0.012961   sd 0.002143   95% range [+0.010435, +0.018843]
      observed  +0.011873   z = -0.51   two-sided p_perm = 0.5577

   -- majority flips --
      null mean +7.396300   sd 2.264298   95% range [+3.000000, +12.000000]
      observed  +9.000000   z = +0.71   two-sided p_perm = 0.4764

   -- RECONCILING WITH THE WILCOXON TEST IN SCRIPT 03 --
      script 03 reported Wilcoxon on per-item d_P(Yes): p = 7.57e-07, which
      looks decisive. The permutation test above says p = 0.5635. They disagree because the
      Wilcoxon treats the 350 items as 350 independent observations. They are not.
      The SAME 19 raters are removed from every item, so there is exactly ONE
      independent draw of a removal set, not 350. The Wilcoxon is pseudo-replicated
      and its p-value is not interpretable as evidence about the filter.
      The permutation test, whose unit of randomisation is the rater set, is the
      correct one. THE WILCOXON RESULT IN SCRIPT 03 IS SUPERSEDED AND SHOULD NOT
      BE QUOTED. What the Wilcoxon does establish, descriptively, is that the
      shift is CONSISTENT in sign across items (207 up, 143 down) - but a
      randomly chosen set of 19 raters produces an equally consistent shift.

   WHY THE DIRECTION EXISTS - composition of the removed group:
      removed raters (n=19)  P(Yes) = 0.3020
      kept raters    (n=104) P(Yes) = 0.3312
      difference = +0.0293
      the filter removes 14/61 men but 5/62 women; men's pool-wide P(Yes) is
      0.3116 vs women's 0.3416, so dropping men tilts the
      surviving pool toward the higher-P(Yes) group.

(2) IS JSD MISLEADING AT LOW ENTROPY?
================================================================================================
   total variation distance, TVD = 0.5*sum|p-q|, has no log term.
      TVD: mean 0.01556, median 0.01403, max 0.04339
      JSD vs entropy:  Spearman -0.0475 (p=0.376)   Pearson -0.2562 (p=1.19e-06)
      TVD vs entropy:  Spearman +0.2665 (p=4.2e-07)   Pearson +0.2502 (p=2.15e-06)

   MECHANISM CHECK: JSD ~= (1/(8 ln2)) * sum_i (q_i-p_i)^2 / p_i ?
      Pearson r(approx, actual JSD) = 0.931012
      max |approx - actual|         = 5.243e-03
      mean relative error           = 0.0530
      -> the second-order form is accurate here, so the 1/p_i weighting is real.

      Spearman(smallest unfiltered cell prob, JSD) = -0.0786 (p=0.142)
      Spearman(smallest unfiltered cell prob, TVD) = +0.1836 (p=0.000555)
      NOTE: min-cell size alone does NOT correlate with JSD, because the size of
      the perturbation D_i varies too. The 1/p_i weighting shows up in the
      approximation above, not in a raw marginal correlation. Reported as such.
